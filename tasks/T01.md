Bug: `Sync._httpDateToEpoch` expects 6 words after the comma, but a real header `Tue, 13 Oct 2026 16:03:27 GMT` gives 5 words (`13 oct 2026 16:03:27 gmt`), so it always returns -1 and the clock offset is never applied.
1. In `lib/sync.dart` rename it to a public `static int httpDateToEpoch(String raw)` (update the one caller). Require `parts.length >= 5`, take the time from `parts[3]` split on `:` (3 integer parts, else return -1). Keep returning -1 for anything unparseable.
2. Create `test/sync_test.dart` using `package:http/testing.dart` `MockClient`. Tests:
   - `httpDateToEpoch('Tue, 13 Oct 2026 16:03:27 GMT') == DateTime.utc(2026,10,13,16,3,27).millisecondsSinceEpoch`
   - `httpDateToEpoch('Sun, 06 Nov 1994 08:49:37 GMT') == DateTime.utc(1994,11,6,8,49,37).millisecondsSinceEpoch`
   - `httpDateToEpoch('garbage') == -1`
   - a `Sync('t', client: MockClient(...))` whose 200 response has header `date: Tue, 13 Oct 2026 16:03:27 GMT` and body `{}`: after `await s.poll()`, `s.offsetMs` is within 2000 ms of `DateTime.utc(2026,10,13,16,3,27).millisecondsSinceEpoch - DateTime.now().millisecondsSinceEpoch`.
